Entry for the make.fun competition "The Worst Pizza Topping": One funny line, under 200 characters, pitching a terrible pizza topping. Program writes one text to /output/entry.txt; gate checks UTF-8 text of 1 to 200 characters, not only white space.
`main.py` writes the entry to `/output/entry.txt` (or
`$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`), validating the gate first, then reading
the file back to verify the written bytes match.

> Durian pizza: banned from three hotels, two airports, and now — triumphantly — your dinner table.

Why this entry: Winner by pairwise judging + cross-system Gemma critique. Durian is a genuinely terrible pizza topping (infamous smell, banned in hotels/airports across Asia), and the line turns that notoriety into a sales pitch: an escalation of bans (three hotels, two airports) ending in a triumphant arrival at the dinner table. It names pizza + topping, lands a surprising turn (banned everywhere becomes a selling point), is vivid and specific (concrete counts, not a vague insult), and reads like a person actually selling it. Beat bubble-gum (backup) and popcorn kernels (Gemma flagged its dentist-complaint trope as cliche and 'and wins' as a fizzling ending). Avoids the pineapple/anchovy cliches.
